# Methodology

## Debt observations and transitions

Current totals are exact Treasury Debt to the Penny observations. Comparable history is Treasury GFDEBTN distributed by FRED. A political transition receives the latest observation on or before its date; because GFDEBTN is quarterly, these are consistent period-end proxies rather than inauguration-day balances.

Congress begins January 3 and a scheduled presidential term begins January 20. The control-at-time view intersects those exact date ranges. Federal fiscal year attribution uses control on October 1 and is labeled contextual—not ownership of the budget. Continuing resolutions, inherited budgets, emergency supplementals, and continuing effects of prior laws are not reassigned without documented law-level evidence.

## Nominal and real dollars

Real value = nominal value × (2026 CPI / observation-date CPI). CPI-U (`CPIAUCSL`) is aligned to the latest monthly observation on or before the debt date. Every real metric is labeled “2026 dollars.” Debt-to-GDP is not separately inflation adjusted when numerator and denominator are both nominal.

## Government control

Periods are the intersections of presidential and congressional service. Classification uses the president, House majority, and Senate majority active in that segment. Cumulative values are always accompanied by duration, observations, and annualized change. Timing and association do not establish causation.

## Forecasting and scenarios

The Forecast Center compares last-value, last-growth, and ten-year CAGR candidates with expanding-window rolling-origin validation. The production baseline is versioned. Empirical 5th/95th percentile validation residuals, widened by square root of horizon, form a 90% interval. The Scenario Lab is explicitly deterministic: next debt = current debt + outlays + interest − receipts. Political selections are context only and never apply party multipliers.

## Required interpretation

Federal debt outcomes reflect decisions made across presidential administrations and Congresses, inherited obligations, economic conditions, interest costs, emergencies, and previously enacted laws. Institutional control alone is not proof of causation.
